Skew Ternary Trees

Knuth apologized for having no time to give his program a better user interface, and hoped someone would write an interactive “app” for playing with its trees and graphs. Here is a first piece of one — Alice the ant’s walk, and the four conjugates it finds.

Theorem. Every family of 2n+2 cyclically equivalent ternary trees contains exactly four skew ternary trees.

Put a bud in every empty slot of a ternary tree. The root node and the root bud have rank 0, and the left, middle, and right children of a node of rank r have ranks r−1, r, and r+1. The tree is skew if no node has negative rank.

The computations are a direct translation of Knuth’s skew-ternary-calc.w: the chapters that parse the tree and build the state chart, and the four chapters that turn a tree into a planar map (from the quad-edge data structure through the correspondence of Del Lungo et al.). On more than 8,900 distinct random trees they reproduce the program’s entire output character for character. The drawings of the maps are not part of the original, which prints only lists of vertices, edges, and faces. Here each map’s barycentric subdivision is built, the boundary of the outer face is pinned to a circle, and every interior node is placed at the average of its neighbors (Tutte); then the nodes are spread apart and the edges straightened, never letting a triangle of the subdivision flip over. The drawings are guaranteed to be free of crossings and stay readable up to about twenty nodes.
